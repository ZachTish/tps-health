# TPS Health 0.6.0

TPS Health 0.6.0 is a backward-compatible food-serving and meal-logging reliability release. It recovers real packaged-food label servings when provider search rows are incomplete, keeps per-100 data honest, and makes creating then logging a meal consume the source tray safely.

## User-visible changes

- Search results without trustworthy serving metadata now say `per 100 g` or `per 100 ml` instead of showing those values as a generic serving. Native labeled gram servings receive a bounded preference only after product-name and brand relevance.
- Selecting a barcode-backed Open Food Facts result performs one cached exact-product enrichment before Add, Choose amount, local-food creation, or recipe selection. Joseph's lavash can therefore resolve from the incomplete search row to `0.5 lavash / 32 g` with the corresponding 60-calorie label values while retaining its descriptive search name.
- Fractional household measures, structured metric quantities, partial nutrition labels, repeated Add actions, and equivalent provider duplicates now retain one coherent serving-and-nutrition basis. Exact-product Retry-After responses use the shared Open Food Facts circuit rather than causing repeated product reads.
- Existing barcode-backed local notes with a generic or known per-100 basis can be refreshed from the exact product label while preserving their local path, name, aliases, notes, and other identity metadata. TPS Health persists the optional nutrition-basis marker on foods it writes or updates.
- Creating a meal replaces only the unchanged captured ingredients with the saved meal. Cancelling or failing the follow-up log leaves the meal queued; a successful log consumes it. Later tray changes and newer pending drafts remain untouched, and duplicate submit guards prevent repeated meal notes or logs.
- A daily-note food insertion remains committed even if the optional rollup refresh or editor-focus follow-up fails. TPS Health warns about that follow-up without presenting the already-written food as retryable.

## Compatibility and migration

- Minimum supported Obsidian version remains 1.12.0.
- Existing settings, food notes, daily-note log lines, plugin APIs, and BRAT installation remain compatible.
- No manual migration is required. Existing food notes without `nutritionBasis` retain their prior serving interpretation; TPS Health adds the marker when it writes a known labeled or per-100 provider basis.

## Validation

- The final declared suite passed 147 of 148 checks with only the intentionally credential-gated live USDA integration check skipped: three Home, two date-owner, 127 provider checks with 126 passed, nine Describe, and seven routed-settings checks. Focused coverage includes honest per-100 fallbacks, exact serving enrichment, fractional measures, coherent provider deduplication, stale local barcode upgrades, 11-/12-/13-/14-digit GTIN equivalence, close-relevance serving priority, meal-tray consumption, partial-log recovery, and draft compare-and-swap ownership.
- The versioned artifact deployed only to the isolated test runtime and was reloaded with **Reload app without saving** in Obsidian 1.13.7. A live `joseph lavash` search placed the labeled USDA result first; **Choose amount** showed `0.5 lavash = 1 serving = 32 g` and 60.2 kcal/P 5 g/C 9 g/F 1.5 g. No food or meal was logged. The existing two-portion Banana tray, `300g egg whites` search text, Scan tab, consumed time, settings, and credentials were preserved; the logger refreshed only its transient draft identity/timestamp. Source and test-runtime artifacts were byte-identical, and production was not accessed or promoted.

## Known limitations

- Provider search indexes can be incomplete or stale. Exact Open Food Facts enrichment runs only on deliberate selection and remains subject to provider availability and rate limits.
- USDA search rows use labeled serving fields when FoodData Central includes them; rows that omit those fields remain explicit per-100 g fallbacks rather than triggering an extra detail request.
- Physical iPhone behavior should receive the normal on-device acceptance check after the BRAT pull.
