# TPS Health 4.2.3

Tested in Obsidian Plugin Test Vault; production installation is separate.

Backward-compatible performance correction; minimum Obsidian 1.12.0.

## Change

Reading-mode navigation and layout sweeps now resolve the connected DOM mount target, then check the existing native-record index for workout membership before requesting a workout snapshot. Ordinary preview notes no longer enumerate every workout session. Missing or disconnected preview targets do neither membership nor projection work.

The existing stale-surface cleanup still runs for mounted ordinary notes. Newly indexed sessions are recognized on the next sweep, saved sets refresh the same surface, duplicates are removed, and archived or ambiguous snapshots remain ineligible. The actual workout projection, Reading renderer, and Live Preview editor lifecycle are unchanged. No new cache, listener, timer, persisted setting, schema, API, note writer, or migration.

## Evidence and focused validation

- Before-fix actual-method regression: 20 ordinary preview leaves × 20 sweeps × 10,000 indexed workouts caused 4,000,000 workout-record visits. After the guard: zero snapshots, zero full-kind enumerations, zero record visits, and 400 current indexed-membership checks.
- Two missing/disconnected preview targets over 20 sweeps: 40 snapshots before, zero afterward; the fixed path also makes zero membership checks.
- Regression coverage retains late membership discovery, saved-set updates, mounted surface identity, stale/duplicate removal, loss and restoration of membership, archived rejection, and ambiguous-identity rejection.
- Focused command: `node --test scripts/test-reading-render-ownership.mjs scripts/test-workout-editor-lifecycle.mjs scripts/test-native-workout-surface.mjs` — 47 passed, zero failed or skipped.
- Versioned full declared suite: 605 passed of 606 checks, one existing optional skip, zero failures. TypeScript and the bundled build passed.
- Separate production-mode build passed and deployed only to Obsidian Plugin Test Vault. Named reload confirmed Health 4.2.3. Source and installed artifact hashes match.
- Standalone TypeScript and diff checks also passed.

## Installed before/after validation

The named Obsidian Plugin Test Vault was verified before each probe. On 4.2.2, real workspace-API navigation with three ordinary Reading leaves caused one Reading-surface sweep and three calls each to `getWorkoutSnapshot` and `getKindRecords` on most switches. Initial one-, two-, and three-preview states caused one, two, and three snapshots respectively. The installed navigation sequence totaled 42 snapshots and 42 full-kind enumerations before the fix, versus zero of both after the 4.2.3 reload. Ordinary note bodies remained unchanged.

An additional installed fixture used the current GCM envelope encoder without starting an active workout or changing settings. A unique ordinary Inbox note initially had no workout membership or surface. Adding complete-session classification through the saved note made it eligible and rendered 5 reps; changing its saved set rendered 6 reps. Removing classification cleared membership and detached the stale surface. Restoring classification restored the surface with 6 reps. The probe waited for real metadata indexing and called the actual Reading-surface update method for deterministic DOM assertions; it did not manually seed Health's index. Its own preview tab was detached, the original active leaf restored, and the synthetic note marked archived and moved directly to `_archive`. The fixed body was preserved, the active-workout state and in-memory settings stayed unchanged, and monitored settings-file hashes remained at their original values.

The desktop window was hidden. Before-fix navigation operations took 8–59 ms and the long-task observer reported no tasks above 50 ms. These are service-level integration and DOM measurements, not foreground click/frame latency. They confirm the removal of unnecessary indexed-history work on the real navigation route and retention of actual workout behavior; they do not establish the cause or resolution of the entire reported multi-second freeze. Physical iPhone, iPad, and Windows latency remain unverified. No production deployment or physical-device installation occurred.
